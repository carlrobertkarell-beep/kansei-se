# Reda Exercise Intelligence

## Product rule
Reda is not an exercise catalogue. The patient should understand why an exercise is in *their* plan and feel that the level matches their actual capacity.

## Three clinical layers
1. **Control & tolerance** — low irritability / low capacity / early re-entry.
2. **Build capacity** — meaningful progressive resistance and task-specific loading.
3. **Performance / return** — higher external load, unilateral demand, rate of force development, plyometric or sport/work-specific tasks where clinically appropriate.

The clinician owns the level. Reda never unlocks progression automatically.

## Exercise family model
Every exercise family should support multiple variants rather than one generic exercise:
- movement target / tissue target
- clinical intent
- starting position and ROM constraint
- external load and equipment
- tempo / isometric / isotonic / eccentric emphasis
- bilateral / unilateral
- stability / coordination demand
- speed / power demand
- regression and progression links
- contraindication / caution metadata
- home / gym availability
- patient-facing rationale: `why this is in your plan`
- clinician-facing rationale and selection notes
- dedicated motion asset for each materially different variant

## Selection model
Inputs should include diagnosis/presentation, irritability, current capacity, prior training exposure, goal, home/gym equipment, floor tolerance, and clinician-selected emphasis. A patient who has trained for years must not receive chair rises simply because the region is "knee".

## Patient experience
For every exercise show:
- exact variant and dose
- **Your level**
- **Why this is in your plan**
- **Your focus** (one high-value cue)
- relationship to the patient's stated goal
- what the clinician will reassess before progression

Avoid gamified automatic progression. Motivation should come from relevance, competence, visible progress, and a plan that feels deliberately selected.

## Content priorities
Build deep families before broad coverage:
1. Knee: PFP, knee OA, patellar tendon, post-meniscus/ACL capacity
2. Shoulder: cuff-related pain, stiffness, scapular/cuff capacity
3. Achilles / calf
4. Lateral hip / GTPS and hip capacity
5. Elbow / forearm
6. Lumbar / cervical directed loading

Each priority area needs credible beginner, intermediate and high-capacity variants plus matching motion assets before it is considered production-ready.

## Acceptance test
A trained patient opening Reda should not be able to say: "This is just the same basic rehab I have already done." The plan should visibly answer: *why this exercise, why this variant, why this dose, and what are we building toward?*
